BlueLab

AI Due Diligence Lab for the Blue Economy

Training the next generation of lawyers to work as sustainability compliance officers — checking sources, documenting reasoning, and using AI responsibly on realistic shipping, offshore energy, and marine industry cases.

1 September 2026 – 31 October 2027 View NWO project page View on GitHub

Overview

Preparing law graduates for the reality of ESG compliance

Dutch law graduates know environmental rules in theory, but employers say they cannot apply them in practice. Meanwhile, companies face a flood of sustainability regulations — EU directives, UN treaties, reporting requirements — that no one can manage without technology. Law firms already use AI to process this paperwork.

BlueLab prepares students for this reality. Working as compliance officers on realistic cases from shipping, offshore energy, and marine industries, students learn to use AI responsibly: checking sources, documenting reasoning, and delivering professional advice. The teaching materials are openly available so other universities can adopt the approach.

Why

Why the blue economy needs AI-literate compliance officers

In the blue economy, sustainability legislation generates vast documentation,[1] crossing multiple frameworks in areas such as biodiversity, offshore energy, pollution, and shipping.[2] Sustainability compliance officers must navigate a fragmented legal landscape,[3] ranging from EU directives to UN treaties, while regulators and clients expect clear, defensible compliance advice. In practice, the volume of documents is beyond what humans can process unaided, and law firms and compliance teams are already turning to AI tools for document triage and obligation mapping.[4]

Graduates leave law school unprepared for the growing field of ESG compliance in the labour market. They study environmental law theoretically, but they are not trained to handle the professional workflows of ESG due diligence, risk analysis, and client reporting that employers demand.[5] With 68% of legal professionals reporting rising demand for ESG-oriented legal expertise,[6] this gap creates a direct mismatch between what the labour market needs and what universities deliver. Because ESG compliance generates vast and fragmented documentation, it cannot be managed without technology. Law firms and compliance teams already rely on AI to triage documents and identify obligations.[4] Nearly three-quarters of lawyers expect to adopt AI in their work in the near future,[7] and the Dutch Bar has stated that trainee lawyers must learn to use and critically assess AI outputs.[8]

Pilot & institutional fit

Embedded in the Legal Analytics course at UM

The Legal Analytics course in the Bachelor of Laws at Maastricht University (UM) serves as the ideal pilot. Embedding the BlueLab here ensures feasibility — existing expertise, motivated and tech-savvy students, institutional support — and scalability to other courses and programmes.

The approach aligns with UM's Sustainability Roadmap 2030,[9] which calls for every graduate to engage with sustainability education and act as a responsible global citizen. Linking sustainability governance with digital skills, the BlueLab translates UM's institutional vision of technology-enhanced, problem-based learning (PBL) into an innovative training ground for ESG compliance in the blue economy, directly preparing graduates for labour market demands.

Innovation

A practice-oriented workflow, not just doctrine or tooling

To our knowledge, no higher-education programme currently integrates AI training as a method for managing ESG compliance in the blue economy. Although some law programmes cover AI methods (e.g. document classification, contract review) and others address ESG as doctrinal knowledge, none merge these into a practice-oriented workflow that reflects how ESG compliance can only be managed at scale with AI.[10][20] This need is clearest in the blue economy, where overlapping regimes require lawyers to produce precise, defensible compliance outputs.[11]

Students work in simulated roles as sustainability compliance officers. Each team receives a case brief describing a company facing maritime sustainability obligations, along with a curated document pack containing EU directives, treaty provisions, and corporate sustainability disclosures.

Case brief & document pack

Teams receive a company facing maritime sustainability obligations, plus EU directives, treaty provisions, and corporate disclosures.

2

AI-assisted triage

Using the BlueLab dashboard, students triage this material with AI tools and identify relevant requirements.

Obligation register

Findings are structured into an obligation register documenting reasoning and sources.

Client memorandum

Teams draft a client-style memorandum with a concrete action plan.

The BlueLab is open and platform-independent. Law firms use proprietary platforms such as Workiva[12] and RepRisk[13] to process ESG disclosures, but each organisation invests in its own system. In BlueLab, students learn AI-supported compliance as a transferable workflow, enabling graduates to adapt to any corporate platform. The approach can thus be replicated in other settings, courses, and institutions.

Expected outcomes

Impact at three levels

Student-level

Participants produce compliance maps, client-style risk memoranda with action plans, and reflective notes on responsible AI use — outputs of direct added value for employability in ESG compliance, sustainability, and corporate responsibility. With rising demand for ESG expertise,[6] BlueLab graduates will stand out in a labour market that urgently seeks these combined skills.

Curricular-level

The project delivers a replicable, open-licensed teaching package — cases, worksheets, rubrics, and a prompt guide — scalable to other law courses, faculties, and universities. Because the method is platform-independent, the package also holds value for other professions such as accountants, engineers, computer scientists, and public administrators.

Societal-level

By equipping future lawyers to translate complex ESG frameworks into actionable compliance advice, BlueLab supports smart governance for the sustainability transition.[14] In the Netherlands, with its ocean-dependent economy, graduates trained in the blue economy will strengthen both the rule of law and national ambitions for a sustainable transition.
